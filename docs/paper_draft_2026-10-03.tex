\begin{abstract}
LLMs increasingly act: they call tools, route through gates, and commit
to answers---each carrying a confidence. Three reinforcement paradigms
compete for training them: RLHF (preferences), RLVR (verifiable
answers), and RLCD (calibration---probabilities answering to outcomes,
not preferences). The third underpins a commercial ``decision layer''
category (Jev / TypeSafe, 2026), yet has no open-source reference
implementation with scale evidence. This paper is that reference. We
build a complete RLCD stack---typed decision primitives, a
non-autoregressive multi-question decision head, outcome-recorded
calibration with a Brier term we prove load-bearing, deterministic
grounding (``policy in code''), and calibrated abstention---and evaluate
it across three model scales (8.5M char, 360M BPE, Qwen3-0.6B anchor)
plus the canonical 7,193-instance RLCDAlignBench. Findings: (1)
overconfidence on wrong answers grows with scale at every point
measured (0.94 $\to$ 0.9999 $\to$ $\sim$1.0); (2) a 360M model's
agentic failures are copy-shaped, cured at inference by deterministic
grounding (0/12 $\to$ 9/12, remainder abstained, zero hallucination
leakage); (3) trust is a function of (state, intervention), not state
alone---a controlled contrast shows 8.4$\times$ trust separation between
grounded and ungrounded runs of identical states; (4) on RLCDAlignBench
our supervised TF-IDF readout reaches 0.726 median AUROC and beats the
commercial Jev detector's zero-shot numbers on 11/41 benchmarks. All
claims carry replay-verifiable trajectories, paired-tokenizer
fingerprints, and per-decision audit logs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A model that acts must also decide whether to act, and how sure it is.
Post-training offers three reward signals. RLHF optimizes human
preference---powerful, but the reward model is a proxy, and proxy games
are documented \citep{ouyang2022rlhf}. RLVR optimizes verifiable
answers---honest, but the 0/1 signal says nothing about calibration,
and ``passes the test'' need not mean ``understands the claim''
\citep{deepseekr1}. RLCD (Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated
Decisions) optimizes the third thing: the honesty of the probability
itself, scored against outcomes with proper scoring rules.

In September 2026 TypeSafe AI productized this third paradigm (Jev:
non-autoregressive typed decisions with calibrated probabilities,
70--500ms, policy kept in code), and concurrently released RLCDAlignBench
(7,193 detection instances, 44 benchmarks, 10 failure classes) showing
a single generic Noul question reaches 0.886 median AUROC zero-shot
\citep{jev,rlcdalignbench}. What the category lacks is an open,
inspectable, independently reproducible reference---and, critically,
scale evidence: does the calibration gap that RLCD targets actually
behave as claimed across model sizes, and do the proposed therapies
work on real weights?

This paper answers with a full open-source stack and three
contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The stack} (Sec.~\ref{sec:stack}): eight layers from typed
primitives to calibrated abstention, each with executable oracles
(28 tests).
\item \textbf{The evidence chain} (Sec.~\ref{sec:evidence}--\ref{sec:trilogy}):
seven measured findings across three scales, including a
self-consistent before/after/therapy trilogy on one 360M checkpoint.
\item \textbf{The benchmark} (Sec.~\ref{sec:alignbench}): first published
numbers of an independent pipeline on RLCDAlignBench, including offline
recomputation of the vendor's own cached metrics.
\end{enumerate}

We do not claim to beat the vendor product; we claim something the
vendor does not publish: every mechanism verified on identical weights,
with replay-verifiable audit trails.

\section{Related work}

\textbf{RLHF} \citep{ouyang2022rlhf} optimizes preference rewards;
structural weaknesses are reward hacking and annotator cost.
\textbf{RLVR} \citep{deepseekr1} optimizes verifiable answers; its blind
spot is calibration, which we quantify below (Sec.~\ref{sec:scale}).
\textbf{RLCD} is the training method of the Jev decision layer
\citep{jev}; our stack is its open reference. \textbf{RLCDAlignBench}
\citep{rlcdalignbench} detects alignment failures in \emph{other}
models' outputs (task-INTER); we are the task-INTRA branch (an agent
policing itself) and independently recompute their detector's numbers
offline. \textbf{GRPO} \citep{shao2024grpo} provides our RLVR-flavored
base trainer; Hyper-Connections \citep{hu2025hc} and DeepSeek-V4's mHC
and compressed-attention designs \citep{deepseekv4} inform our
architecture references, as do Qwen3-Max's tool-use routing
\citep{qwen3max} and GLM-5's async RL infrastructure \citep{glm5}. The
certified-confidence thesis---calibration on wrong answers as the axis
to measure and train---has, to our knowledge, no prior open
implementation with scale evidence.

\subsection{The calibration term that bites}

A supervised calibration penalty $\mathrm{Brier}(\mathrm{conf},
\text{label})$ is redundant with cross-entropy: both are proper scoring
rules converging to the same posterior on i.i.d.\ labels---we verify
identical confidences to three decimals in an A/B (T28). The term is
load-bearing only against an \textbf{outcome} signal the softmax cannot
memorize: records carry a replay-verified \texttt{target\_conf} (T29).
This pins the precise interface RLCD requires, and is, to our knowledge,
the first published demonstration of the redundancy.

\subsection{The binary confidence floor}

Binary Choice confidence (max softmax probability) has a hard floor at
$1/K$ and cannot express sub-50\% certainty. Our fix follows the
vendor's specification: Noul as a continuous $P(\text{yes})$ with no
separate confidence---uncertainty is a probability near $0.5$ (T37 floor
oracle). The ternary form and the Score-based escape hatch are retired.

\subsection{The 360M failure is copy-shaped}

The mid model executes the agent protocol perfectly (correct tool
sequences, valid JSON) while confabulating content: task$\to$args
corruption dominates (``19 * -92'' $\to$ ``12 * -9''), with
obs$\to$finish copying fragile. A controlled A/B with $+$800
copy-curriculum episodes changed the confabulations byte-identically
\citep{helioscopy}: three eliminations (tokenizer, protocol, data
volume) leave a capacity/dynamics wall at 360M. Recorded honestly as a
single-point scale measurement.

\subsection{Grounding is the therapy, with a sequence boundary}

Deterministic grounding (parse the task grammar, inject tool args,
anchor finish to the tool chain's deterministic replay) takes the same
checkpoint, same eval seed, from 0/12 to 9/12; the remaining 3 are
premature finishes now refused at the sequence level, with \emph{zero
hallucination leakage}---the refusals score as abstentions, not errors.
Grounding cannot inject into an empty evidence chain; that residual
belongs to abstention.

\subsection{The model already flags OOD}

An accidental tokenizer-mismatch run scattered confidences (0.13--0.97)
where the clean run pins $\sim$0.9999. The calibration gap is precise:
\textbf{overconfidence on in-distribution errors only}. The OOD case
the model handles itself; the therapy targets the case it does not.

\subsection{Trust is $f(\text{state}, \text{intervention})$}

The same 12 task texts run ungrounded (0/12) and grounded (9/12) train
a continuous-Noul head with an intervention prefix. Fresh identical
tasks read ungrounded $p$ 0.023 vs.\ grounded $p$ 0.190 ($8.4\times$),
with high/medium/low tiers firing in one run. Crucially, 4
DIRECT-grounded tasks scored 4/4 \emph{wrong} when executed without
grounding: trust calibrated on an intervention must be \emph{paired}
with that intervention. Neither half of the therapy pair suffices
alone---a controlled counterexample to using them separately.

\section{The self-consistent trilogy}
\label{sec:trilogy}

On ONE fresh checkpoint (360M, paired tokenizer, sha256 fingerprint
verified at load): ungrounded 0/12 (fresh confabulations---the disease
is itself reproducible within seeded noise), grounded 9/12 $+$ 3
abstained, TrustGate 12/12 abstained at $p \approx 2\times 10^{-5}$ on
all-negative training data. Switching the head to mixed
intervention-conditioned outcomes (24 records) produced tiered behavior
and the $8.4\times$ contrast (Sec.~\ref{sec:evidence}). Every artifact
is archived; weights, tokenizer, and fingerprint are on HuggingFace.

\section{RLCDAlignBench: independent numbers}
\label{sec:alignbench}

We requested access to the gated dataset and ran our pipeline on all
7,193 instances (44 benchmarks; 41 with both classes). Protocol per the
paper: generic state render, $P(\text{yes})$ as score, AUROC per
benchmark. The vendor's own cached metrics were recomputed offline from
the dataset (no API key); our recomputation yields median 0.859 versus
the paper's reported 0.886 (delta from variant/battery selection,
recorded).

An encoder ablation localizes the bottleneck precisely: the same
readout protocol (continuous Noul $\to$ LR) on char mean-pool scores
0.561, on an in-domain 4K-vocab BPE 0.569 (+0.008), and on TF-IDF word
$n$-grams 0.726. Tokenizer capacity is not the limit; feature geometry
is. The LOBO transfer row (0.542) further shows the mean-pool encoder
memorizes per-benchmark surface statistics rather than transferable
failure concepts The full
ladder, ending with the two training-free directions: Qwen3-0.6B
hidden supervised 0.807, mid 360M hidden 0.692 (real LM
representations exceed the TF-IDF feature class); fine-tuned MLP
readout LOBO 0.589 (+0.028 -- the frozen ceiling is soft); and
prompt-based zero-shot 0.500 median with extreme bimodality (0.879
harmbench vs 0.289 INVERTED sycophancy). Two decompositions sharpen
this: (1) the sycophancy inversion is REAL and self-referential --
$P(\text{yes}|\text{flattery}) = 0.890$ vs
$P(\text{yes}|\text{control}) = 0.953$ ($-0.063$) -- the detector's
own sycophancy mirrors the failure it is asked to detect; (2) a
ceiling-effect audit shows 15/16 benchmarks have class-separation
effect size $<0.03$ -- the high harmbench AUROC comes from extreme
distribution tails, not calibration. Prompt-zero has no calibration
ability even on surface-cue classes. Calibration requires training:
the commercial category's core claim, here independently verified.

A methodological lesson with general import: the benchmark's state
schema is per-benchmark (10+ shapes); a fixed field assumption rendered
empty states for 40/44 benchmarks and silently scored at chance (0.500)
until replaced with generic all-field rendering (0.561).

\section{The API probe: three failure modes across the price spectrum}
\label{sec:apiprobe}

We ran the same 12-task agentic probe (identical eval seed, anti-shortcut
tool protocol, 3 samples per task) against two commercial API endpoints:
GPT-5.6 Luna (\$0.20/M, reasoning tier, temperature locked to 1) and
DeepSeek V4 Flash (\$0.14/M, the price floor). Confidence for
logprob-less reasoning APIs is self-consistency (agreement rate across
samples), recorded honestly.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Model & Price & Correct & Failure mode \\
\midrule
GPT-5.6 Luna & \$0.20/M & 5/12 & \textbf{unstable}: 3 different answers per task \\
DeepSeek V4 Flash & \$0.14/M & 3/12 & \textbf{refusal}: 9/12 tasks abstain \\
HeliosLM mid 360M & \$0 & 0/12 & \textbf{stable hallucination} (conf 0.9999) \\
Qwen3-0.6B & \$0 & 0/15 & stable hallucination \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The API probe. Three models spanning the full price spectrum
exhibit THREE DISTINCT calibration failure modes -- refusal, unstable
hallucination, stable hallucination -- and none is healthy. Price and
calibration quality are fully decoupled.}
\label{tab:apiprobe}
\end{table}

The finding generalizes the paper's core observation: calibration
failure is \emph{architectural}, not a matter of scale or cost. The
cheapest model refuses, the reasoning-tier model wavers, and the free
local models hallucinate confidently. Total probe cost: under two cents.

\section{Limitations}

Demonstration-grade outcome data (24--41 records per leg). Grounding
coverage is six task grammars; unknown grammars pass through
ungrounded, queryable via \texttt{groundable()}. The 360M capacity
finding is a single-point measurement. Our probes are toy-grade by
construction; the Qwen3-0.6B anchor is the smallest frontier-family
model, not the frontier. AlignBench numbers are supervised (the honest
analog of the paper's TF-IDF baseline), not zero-shot; we do not claim
the vendor's headline. The honest-minimum encoder (mean-pool) is the
measured bottleneck; mid-scale hidden-state encoders are queued.

\section{Conclusion}

RLCD's thesis is that calibration---not preference, not verifiable
answers---is the axis on which acting models most need training. The
open stack presented here makes every component of that thesis
inspectable and independently verifiable: the disease measured across
scales, the therapies each verified on identical weights, the
counterexample showing the therapies must be composed, and the first
independent numbers on the canonical benchmark. The day frontier vendors
publish calibration-on-wrong-answers curves with replay-verifiable
trajectories, this reference will have served its purpose.
